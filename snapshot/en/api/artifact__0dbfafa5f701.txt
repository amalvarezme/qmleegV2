# Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Network with Data Re-uploading for EEG-based ADHD Classification

## 1. Problem Formulation and Clinical Scope

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) diagnosis traditionally relies on behavioral questionnaires, which are subject to clinical observer bias. Electroencephalography (EEG) provides a high-temporal-resolution physiological measurement of brain electrical activity. Under the international 10-20 standard montage, multichannel recordings capture rhythmic oscillations across standardized frequency bands: Delta (0.5-4 Hz), Theta (4-8 Hz), Alpha (8-12 Hz), and Beta (12-30 Hz). In pediatric ADHD, elevated Theta power alongside reduced Beta activity in fronto-central electrodes constitutes the established Theta/Beta ratio (TBR) biomarker.

However, multichannel EEG suffers from substantial inter-subject variability, low signal-to-noise ratio, and volume conduction artifacts. This proposal formulates a hybrid quantum-classical learning architecture wherein a classical convolutional backbone condenses spatial-temporal correlations into a compact latent vector, and a parameterized quantum circuit with circular Data Re-uploading (DRU) operates as an expressive non-linear classifier.

## 2. Classical Spatial-Temporal Feature Extraction

Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times T}$ denote a raw EEG epoch segment comprising $C$ recording channels (with $C = 19$ under the 10-20 system) and $T$ discrete temporal sampling points. The classical feature extraction backbone $f_{\phi}: \mathbb{R}^{C \times T} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ maps the continuous EEG epoch into a low-dimensional feature representation matching the qubit width of the quantum register:

$$
x = f_{\phi}(X) \in [-\pi, \pi]^d, \quad \text{with } d = N_q = 4
\tag{1}
$$

The dimension $d$ is deliberately constrained to $N_q = 4$ to permit direct qubit allocation without barren plateaus. The four latent dimensions correspond to distinct physiological features: frontal Theta power, central Beta power, global Theta/Beta ratio, and inter-hemispheric frontal coherence.

## 3. Quantum Data Re-uploading Classifier Architecture

### 3.1 Data Re-uploading Unitary Operator

Rather than employing a single-shot state preparation circuit, the quantum classifier interleaves data encoding blocks with trainable variational transformations across $L$ successive layers. The global unitary evolution operator $U(x, \theta)$ acting on the reference state $|0^{\otimes N_q}\rangle$ is defined as:

$$
U(x, \theta) = \prod_{l=1}^L W_l(\theta_l) \, S_l(x)
\tag{2}
$$

The data encoding operator $S_l(x)$ applies single-qubit rotations parameterized by affine transformations of the input vector $x$, where $\alpha_{l,j}$ and $\beta_{l,j}$ are layer-specific scaling and bias parameters:

$$
S_l(x) = \bigotimes_{j=1}^{N_q} R_y(\alpha_{l,j} x_j + \beta_{l,j})
\tag{3}
$$

Each variational layer $W_l(\theta_l)$ applies parameterized single-qubit rotations followed by an entangling operation:

$$
W_l(\theta_l) = V_{\text{ent}} \cdot \bigotimes_{j=1}^{N_q} R_z(\theta_{l,j}^{(2)}) R_y(\theta_{l,j}^{(1)})
\tag{4}
$$

### 3.2 Circular Entanglement Topology

To ensure non-local correlations and maximum bipartite entanglement across the 4-qubit register while preventing boundary asymmetry, $V_{\text{ent}}$ is configured with a closed periodic boundary (circular CNOT ring):

$$
V_{\text{ent}} = \left( \prod_{j=1}^{N_q - 1} \text{CNOT}_{j, j+1} \right) \cdot \text{CNOT}_{N_q, 1}
\tag{5}
$$

By virtue of the $L$-fold re-uploading defined in (Ec. 2), the quantum circuit acts as a truncated Fourier series whose accessible spectrum extends up to harmonic degree $L$.

### 3.3 Measurement and Decision Boundary

Expectation values of the Pauli-$Z$ observable are measured individually on each qubit $j \in \{1, \dots, N_q\}$:

$$
\langle Z_j \rangle(x, \theta) = \langle 0^{\otimes N_q} | U^\dagger(x, \theta) \, Z_j \, U(x, \theta) | 0^{\otimes N_q} \rangle \in [-1, 1]
\tag{6}
$$

The vector of expectation values $\langle Z \rangle = [\langle Z_1 \rangle, \dots, \langle Z_{N_q} \rangle]^T$ is projected onto a binary diagnostic decision through a classical linear combination followed by the logistic sigmoid activation:

$$
\hat{y} = \sigma\left( \sum_{j=1}^{N_q} w_j \langle Z_j \rangle(x, \theta) + b \right)
\tag{7}
$$

where $\hat{y} \in (0, 1)$ represents the predicted posterior probability of ADHD diagnosis ($y = 1$) versus healthy control ($y = 0$).

## 4. Loss Function and Parameter Optimization

The joint parameters of the hybrid network, including the classical weights $\phi$, the quantum rotation parameters $\theta = \{\theta_l\}_{l=1}^L$, the encoding scales $\alpha, \beta$, and the output weights $w, b$, are trained end-to-end via regularized binary cross-entropy:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\phi, \theta, w, b) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ y^{(i)} \log \hat{y}^{(i)} + (1 - y^{(i)}) \log(1 - \hat{y}^{(i)}) \right] + \lambda \|\theta\|_2^2
\tag{8}
$$

Optimization proceeds using classical gradient descent with the parameter-shift rule applied to obtain exact analytical gradients for all quantum circuit parameters.

## 5. Clinical Interpretability via Analytical Gradients

To verify clinical validity, the decision saliency with respect to the input EEG signal is derived analytically using the chain rule across the classical and quantum layers:

$$
\mathcal{I}(c, t) = \left| \sum_{k=1}^{N_q} \frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial x_k} \frac{\partial f_{\phi}(X)_k}{\partial X_{c,t}} \right|
\tag{9}
$$

Because the partial derivatives $\frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial x_k}$ are computed via (Ec. 7) and the parameter-shift rule on (Ec. 6), the saliency map $\mathcal{I}(c, t)$ exposes the precise contribution of each electrode $c$ and time sample $t$. This provides a direct clinical audit mechanism to confirm that the hybrid model leverages physiologically established fronto-central regions (Fp1, Fp2, F3, F4, Fz, Cz) rather than background noise artifacts.
