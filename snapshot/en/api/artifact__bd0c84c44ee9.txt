# Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Network with Data Re-uploading for EEG-based ADHD Classification

## Experimental Protocol and Empirical Validation Plan

Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Network with Data Re-uploading for EEG-based ADHD Classification.

Experimental Protocol and Empirical Validation Plan.

**Dataset:** IEEE DataPort "EEG Data for ADHD / Control Children" (Motie Nasrabadi et al., 2020, DOI 10.21227/rzfh-zn36, https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/eeg-data-adhd-control-children [verified: 2026-10-24]): 121 children recorded (61 ADHD, 60 controls), 120 retained after removing subject v56p, 19 channels at 128 Hz, 2.0 s epochs with 50% overlap, subject-independent stratified group split on subject ID; mirrored for remote runs as https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/danizo/eeg-dataset-for-adhd [verified: 2026-10-24]

**Validation scheme:** Stratified 5-fold group cross-validation on subject IDs with 5 seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 1024) and 5 repetitions of the full cross-validation, giving 25 fold-by-seed scores per arm; pairwise comparisons by the corrected resampled t-test (Nadeau and Bengio, 2003), comparisons of three or more arms by the Friedman test with Holm post-hoc, Holm-Bonferroni correction within each experiment, alpha = 0.05, each result reported with its effect size and 95% confidence interval

### Executive Summary and Scope
This document defines the formal experimental protocol to validate the hybrid quantum-classical neural network with data re-uploading (DRU) proposed in [research-concept-hybrid-quantum-classical-neural-network-with-data-re-uploadi-r02.md](proposals/research-concept-hybrid-quantum-classical-neural-network-with-data-re-uploadi-r02.md). The plan tests whether interleaving classical spatial-temporal feature extraction with a multi-layer parameterized quantum circuit provides superior non-linear classification and physiological interpretability on pediatric EEG data without suffering from barren plateaus or data leakage.

### Statistical Analysis Plan

Every experiment names its test, its correction, its effect size and its confidence interval before anything runs. Folds share training subjects, so a plain paired test over fold scores underestimates the variance; and a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over five folds cannot reach p < 0.05 at all, because its smallest exact two-sided p is 0.0625. The tests below are chosen from each experiment's design. The non-inferiority margin against EEGNet is delta = 0.03 of subject-level Balanced Accuracy, fixed before any run.

| Experiment | Design | Test | Correction | Effect size | Confidence interval |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| E1 | Three arms on the same folds, plus non-inferiority against EEGNet | Friedman over the fold-by-seed blocks, then pairwise corrected resampled t-test; McNemar on subject-level out-of-fold decisions; DeLong for AUC-ROC; one-sided corrected resampled t-test for non-inferiority to EEGNet at the declared margin | Holm | Cohen's d and Cliff's delta | Corrected-t interval and paired bootstrap |
| E2 | Three topologies on the same folds | Friedman with Holm post-hoc and a critical-difference diagram | Holm | Cliff's delta | Paired bootstrap |
| E3 | Ordered factor (depth L) | Page's trend test for gradient variance and accuracy over L; regression of log gradient variance on L | Holm across the two endpoints | Page's L statistic and regression slope | Slope interval |
| E4 | Repeated measures per subject | Saliency aggregated per subject, then Wilcoxon signed-rank (or permutation test) over subjects | Holm across the Theta and Beta bands | Rank-biserial correlation | Bootstrap over subjects |
| E5 | Paired independent replicates, each a freshly generated toy set | Wilcoxon signed-rank over the paired replicates; regression of Balanced Accuracy on the label-noise rate | Holm within the confirmatory family | Rank-biserial correlation and regression slope | Bootstrap over replicates and slope interval |

### Proof of Concept and Full Runs

The proof of concept runs locally on the seeded toy set: one fold and one seed per arm, with the same optimizer, schedule and stopping rule as the full runs, to check that every measurement lands and that training converges. A short timing probe (one epoch of one fold per arm) precedes every pilot and every full run, and its measured seconds per epoch give the processing-time estimate reported before anything is launched.

The full runs execute the whole validation scheme on remote GPU notebooks, on Kaggle or on Google Colab, never locally. Each account belongs to one person of the team (one account per person per service), and a Colab account takes part only on a paid tier, because the Colab FAQ disallows "using multiple accounts to work around access or resource usage restrictions" and distributed workers on free runtimes (https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html [verified: 2026-10-25]); Kaggle staff state that "the one account per person applies to all Kaggle site use" (https://www.kaggle.com/discussions/general/192527 [verified: 2026-10-25]). The campaign is split only along the fold-by-seed axis, never by arm, each unit's seed derived from its unit identifier; every account first passes a rehearsal on the pinned commit, and a split across accounts is used only when the measured speed-up justifies it. All shards of one campaign run on one service and one accelerator model, and each shard records an environment stamp (accelerator, CUDA, PyTorch, commit, dataset preparation version) that must be identical across shards before results are merged. The operator alone authorizes a launch.

Both stages record, per run, the convergence history (training and validation loss, balanced accuracy and learning rate per epoch), and the dashboard shows the convergence curves as mean and standard deviation across seeds. Every experiment's significance results are written as one record per comparison (test, statistic, p-value, adjusted p-value, correction, effect size, confidence interval, n, alpha).

The first local pilot (one fold, one seed, toy data, 30 epochs) measured about 2.25 s per epoch per arm on CPU and showed every arm still improving at the last epoch, with subject posteriors close to 0.5; the epoch budget and stopping rule below replace that budget, and the pilot is repeated under them before any full run.

### Hyperparameter Search

The learning rate, the two regularization coefficients, the mini-batch size and the backbone dropout have no published defaults for this architecture, and the comparison with the baselines is fair only if every arm receives the same tuning effort. A hyperparameter search therefore precedes the full runs, with Optuna (https://doi.org/10.1145/3292500.3330701 [verified: 2026-10-25]). It is nested: inside each outer fold it searches over the training subjects only, scoring each trial by the subject-weighted loss on the inner validation subjects, so the outer test subjects are never seen. Every arm, the external reference arms included, receives the same budget of 30 trials per outer fold, drawn by a TPE sampler with seed 42 and pruned by a median rule on the validation loss; the search runs on seed 42 and the selected values are then frozen per outer fold for all five seeds of the full runs. The independent variables the experiments test are never tuned: the re-uploading depth, the entanglement topology, the number of qubits and the encoding scale stay as each experiment declares them, and E2 and E3 reuse the values selected for the Hybrid DRU. Hyperparameter importance is estimated by fANOVA with seed 42 (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/hutter14.html [verified: 2026-10-25]), checked against PED-ANOVA, and any disagreement in their ranking is reported; contour plots of the objective over the three most important hyperparameters and the optimization history are reported per arm and fold. Importance and contours describe the search; they are not hypothesis tests. The search runs remotely under the same rules as the full runs, its processing time is estimated from the timing probe before it is launched, and the operator alone authorizes it.

### Data Sources per Stage

- **Proof of concept:** a family of seeded synthetic EEG toy sets generated by the datasets skill (`synth --kind eeg`), each replicate with 170 freshly generated subjects (60 for training, 10 for validation and 100 for testing, one label per subject, balanced within each group), 12 epochs per subject, 19 channels and 256 samples per epoch at 128 Hz, so that one test subject moves the subject-level Balanced Accuracy by 0.02 rather than the 0.1 step a 20-subject set allows. Each epoch carries narrowband Theta (4-8 Hz), Beta (13-30 Hz) and Alpha (8-12 Hz) activity over an aperiodic 1/f background (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-00744-x [verified: 2026-10-25]), mixed across neighbouring channels to imitate volume conduction, with frontal eye-blink artifacts in a fraction of the epochs and the class effect confined to the fronto-central channels. The class follows one of the three subject-level rules E5 declares (monotone, interaction, frequency-two), and the monotone rule is graded by a between-subject separation d′ with a known ceiling. The local pilot that checks the pipeline runs on the monotone rule with d′ = 3.0 and no label noise. The generating parameters and seed are recorded beside each set, so every proof-of-concept run is reproducible bit for bit.
- **Full experiments:** the dataset declared above, read from its remote mirror, plus one official preprocessed version (notch and band-pass filtering, common average reference, 2.0 s epochs with 50% overlap, and the stratified group folds on subject ID) published by the datasets skill as a private remote dataset that every remote notebook mounts as its input. The source license is recorded before publication, and the preparation manifest carries the source checksum, so the proof of concept and the full experiments read exactly the same preparation.
- **Comparison methods without public code:** the QEEGNet circuit and the spectral SVM-RBF reference are reimplemented from their papers by the replication stretch of the implementation, with every hyperparameter tied to a section or equation of its paper or marked unspecified; the published baselines (EEGNet, ADHDeepNet) run from their own repositories after their correspondence with the paper is checked.

## Experiment [exp:E1] — Hybrid Classification and Fourier Expressivity

- **Claims Sustained:** [claims:2] [claims:3] [claims:7] [claims:8]
- **Hypothesis:** The hybrid architecture combining classical convolutional embedding with circular data re-uploading (L=3, 4 qubits) provides superior non-linear representation compared to single-shot angle encoding and to a classical linear baseline operating on the identical 4-dimensional representation.
- **Independent Variables:** Classifier architecture (Hybrid DRU L=3 vs. Single-shot angle encoding vs. Classical linear probe), with EEGNet and ADHDeepNet as external reference arms.
- **Dependent Variables:** Subject-level and epoch-level classification performance (Balanced Accuracy, Sensitivity, Specificity, F1-score, AUC-ROC).
- **Controlled Variables:** Preprocessing pipeline, channel montage (19 channels, CAR, 0.5-60 Hz), epoch length (2.0 s, 256 samples), training folds, optimizer and training budget of the Hyperparameters section (AdamW, at most 100 epochs with early stopping), each arm's remaining hyperparameters selected per outer fold by the nested search.
- **Protocol:** Models are trained using regularized binary cross-entropy on the training folds. Evaluation is performed strictly on unseen subjects across the 5 stratified group folds and 5 seeds. Epoch predictions are aggregated to subject-level decisions via mean posterior probability thresholding. The three arms are compared by the Friedman test over the 25 fold-by-seed blocks; the Hybrid DRU is then compared with each comparator by the corrected resampled t-test, Holm-corrected, with Cohen's d and its corrected-t interval; McNemar on subject-level out-of-fold decisions and DeLong on AUC-ROC complement the fold-level test. Against EEGNet, the strongest published classical baseline, the Hybrid DRU is tested for non-inferiority: the one-sided corrected resampled t-test over the same 25 fold-by-seed blocks rejects that the Hybrid DRU's subject-level Balanced Accuracy is lower than EEGNet's by the margin delta = 0.03 or more, reported with the one-sided 95% confidence bound of the difference.
- **Success criterion:** Balanced Accuracy on subject-level test evaluation reaches at least 0.85 and the Hybrid DRU is superior to both single-shot angle encoding and the classical linear probe by the corrected resampled t-test with Holm-adjusted p < 0.05, and non-inferior to EEGNet: the lower one-sided 95% confidence bound of the Balanced Accuracy difference (Hybrid DRU minus EEGNet) lies above -0.03.
- **Priority:** core
- **Estimated Cost:** 12 GPU hours on NVIDIA A100 / Tesla T4.

## Experiment [exp:E2] — Entanglement Topology Ablation

- **Claims Sustained:** [claims:5]
- **Hypothesis:** Circular CNOT entanglement topology provides maximum non-local bipartite state connectivity across the 4-qubit register, yielding higher diagnostic discriminability than linear nearest-neighbor entanglement or separable non-entangled single-qubit rotations.
- **Independent Variables:** Entanglement circuit topology (Circular CNOT ring vs. Linear open chain vs. Separable identity V_ent = I).
- **Dependent Variables:** Validation loss convergence, subject-level Balanced Accuracy, and F1-score.
- **Controlled Variables:** Identical classical feature extractor weights, L=3 layers, 4 qubits, identical initialization seeds.
- **Protocol:** Each topological variant is trained on the same 5-fold group cross-validation splits and 5 seeds. The three variants are compared by the Friedman test over the 25 fold-by-seed blocks, followed by Holm post-hoc comparisons and a critical-difference diagram; each pairwise difference is reported with Cliff's delta and a paired bootstrap interval.
- **Success criterion:** Circular entanglement topology demonstrates a higher subject-level F1-score by at least 0.03 compared to the separable non-entangled circuit, with Holm-adjusted post-hoc p < 0.05.
- **Priority:** ablation
- **Estimated Cost:** 6 GPU hours.

## Experiment [exp:E3] — Re-uploading Depth and Barren Plateau Analysis

- **Claims Sustained:** [claims:2] [claims:8]
- **Hypothesis:** Expressivity in the accessible Fourier spectrum increases with re-uploading depth L up to an optimal bound, beyond which gradient variance decays (barren plateau effect) and causes training stagnation or overfitting on validation folds.
- **Independent Variables:** Re-uploading depth L in {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}.
- **Dependent Variables:** Sample gradient variance of circuit parameters Var[grad_theta L], training epochs to convergence, test Balanced Accuracy.
- **Controlled Variables:** 4 qubits, circular entanglement, the hyperparameters selected for the Hybrid DRU by the nested search, identical fold splits.
- **Protocol:** The gradient variance is estimated across the initialization landscape over 100 random parameter initializations per depth. A decay is tested by regressing the logarithm of the gradient variance on L and by Page's trend test across the ordered depths. Models are then trained and evaluated on the 5 group folds and 5 seeds; the trend of test Balanced Accuracy over L is tested by Page's trend test, and the depth with the best accuracy is compared with its neighbours by Holm-corrected post-hoc tests.
- **Success criterion:** The optimal re-uploading depth L identifies the trade-off point where validation loss reaches a minimum before gradient variance decays by more than one order of magnitude, with the regression slope's 95% confidence interval excluding zero.
- **Priority:** ablation
- **Estimated Cost:** 8 GPU hours.

## Experiment [exp:E4] — Clinical Interpretability via Analytical Gradients

- **Claims Sustained:** [claims:9]
- **Hypothesis:** Analytical gradient saliency derived from the classical backbone and quantum parameter-shift rule concentrates its mass on electrophysiologically relevant fronto-central channels and Theta/Beta frequency components rather than posterior noise.
- **Independent Variables:** Region of interest electrode groups (Fronto-central: Fp1, Fp2, F3, F4, Fz, Cz vs. Temporal-occipital: T3, T4, T5, T6, O1, O2).
- **Dependent Variables:** Relative saliency ratio and spectral band energy distribution of the saliency tensor I(c, t).
- **Controlled Variables:** Trained Hybrid DRU model (L=3), input epochs correctly classified as ADHD.
- **Protocol:** The saliency map I(c, t) is computed via exact chain rule across the quantum expectation values and classical feature maps for all test epochs. Saliency is first averaged per subject, so each subject contributes one value per region and band and overlapping epochs are never counted as independent observations. Regions are then compared by the Wilcoxon signed-rank test over subjects (a permutation test as a check), Holm-corrected across the Theta and Beta bands, with the rank-biserial correlation and a bootstrap interval over subjects. After training, each of the four learned features is contrasted post hoc with the biomarkers the ADHD literature reports, computed from the raw signal of the same subjects: frontal Theta power, central Beta power, the Theta/Beta ratio and inter-hemispheric frontal coherence (https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054712460087 [verified: 2026-10-25]). Each contrast is a Spearman correlation over subjects with a permutation test, the sixteen feature-by-biomarker tests corrected by the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure (https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x [verified: 2026-10-25]), and a bootstrap interval over subjects. The contrast describes what the network learned; nothing in the architecture or the loss imposes it.
- **Success criterion:** The mean per-subject analytical gradient saliency allocated to fronto-central electrode channels (Fp1, Fp2, F3, F4, Fz, Cz) in the Theta and Beta frequency bands is statistically greater (Wilcoxon signed-rank over subjects, Holm-adjusted p < 0.01) than the saliency allocated to posterior temporal-occipital channels.
- **Priority:** core
- **Estimated Cost:** 4 GPU hours.

## Experiment [exp:E5] — Controlled Synthetic Capacity and Robustness

- **Claims Sustained:** [claims:2] [claims:3] [claims:7]
- **Hypothesis:** With a fixed feature front end, the re-uploading readout (L=3) represents a class rule that needs Fourier frequency two in one feature, which single-shot angle encoding (L=1) cannot represent; when the class signal is monotone every arm stays close to the known ceiling, and its degradation with the training label-noise rate is measured.
- **Independent Variables:** Classifier arm (Hybrid DRU L=3, Single-shot angle encoding L=1, Classical linear probe); feature regime (end-to-end backbone as in E1, or a fixed front end); class rule (monotone, interaction, frequency-two); between-subject separation d′ (3.0, 1.5, 1.0) for the monotone rule; training label-noise rate (0%, 10%, 20% of the training subjects).
- **Dependent Variables:** Subject-level Balanced Accuracy over the 100 fresh test subjects of each replicate; epoch-level Balanced Accuracy; training and validation loss per epoch.
- **Controlled Variables:** The toy-set generator and its parameters, 60 training, 10 validation and 100 test subjects per replicate, the optimizer, schedule and stopping rule of the Hyperparameters section with the proof-of-concept defaults for every arm, except the fixed regime's training budget declared below (E5 runs no hyperparameter search, so every arm receives the same tuning effort), 4 qubits with circular entanglement, and test labels that are always clean.
- **Fixed-regime Training Budget:** In the fixed regime every arm trains with AdamW at an initial learning rate of 0.01, the upper end of the range the Hyperparameters section declares for the search, with cosine annealing over a maximum of 200 epochs and early stopping with patience 20 on the subject-weighted validation loss; the mini-batch size, the readout penalty and the remaining settings keep the proof-of-concept defaults, and no normalization or dropout is added before the readout, so the readout stays Eq. (7). The end-to-end regime keeps the proof-of-concept budget (0.001, at most 100 epochs, patience 10), under which its pipeline check holds. The fixed regime needs the larger step because its readout receives expectation values in [-1, 1] and starts from weights near 0.1, so at 0.001 the readout weights grow too slowly for a 100-epoch budget.
- **Protocol Amendment (v06):** In the run of the v05 protocol the fixed regime's pipeline check failed for the Single-shot arm at every monotone separation (mean subject-level Balanced Accuracy 0.833, 0.694 and 0.546 against ceilings 0.933, 0.773 and 0.691), and most fixed-regime runs of every arm reached the 100-epoch cap while still improving. Two diagnostics on the same replicates located the cause: clipping the validation and test features to the fitted range changed no subject decision, which rules out the min-max encoding, while a longer budget raised the Single-shot arm's accuracy. The budget above was then selected on separate generator seeds (101 to 108), by validation loss, convergence before the cap and training stability, among learning rates from 0.001 to 0.03, a mini-batch size of 16, a readout-specific learning rate, a parameter-free normalization, dropout and a larger readout penalty; normalization and dropout were set aside because they change Eq. (7) without lowering the validation loss, and warm-up and gradient clipping because they changed nothing measurable at 0.01. The success criterion, its tolerance, the ceilings and the confirmatory family are unchanged, and the v05 results are reported as the original run beside the rerun under this budget, whatever the rerun shows.
- **Protocol:** The three class rules act on subject-level latent variables that scale the Theta and Beta amplitudes of the fronto-central channels. Monotone: the subject's log Theta/Beta offset is drawn from a unit-variance Gaussian centred at plus or minus d′/2 by class, so the Bayes ceiling of subject-level Balanced Accuracy is Φ(d′/2), about 0.93, 0.77 and 0.69 for the three separations. Interaction: independent Gaussian Theta and Beta deviations, class one when both deviate in the same direction; the log Theta/Beta ratio then carries no class information, and on noiseless latents the best linear rule reaches a Balanced Accuracy of 0.625 while a degree-one trigonometric readout with cross-feature products reaches 1.0. Frequency-two: a Theta deviation drawn uniformly over one period, class one where the cosine of twice the deviation is positive; on noiseless latents the best linear rule reaches 0.625, a degree-one trigonometric readout 0.75 and a degree-two readout 1.0. These ceilings are properties of the rules, computed before any run, and trial noise only lowers them. The degree argument follows from the spectrum of data re-uploading models (https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.103.032430 [verified: 2026-10-25]; https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2020-02-06-226 [verified: 2026-10-25]): with unit-scale R_y encoding repeated L times, each feature enters the model output as a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most L. In the end-to-end regime the backbone of E1 is trained jointly with each readout. In the fixed regime the backbone is replaced by four fixed features per epoch, the log Theta and log Beta power of the frontal and of the central channels, mapped by a min-max transform fitted on the training subjects onto the interval from -π to π; the trainable encoding bias absorbs any offset, so only the readout differs between arms, which separates the readout's expressivity from what a trained backbone learns by itself. Label noise is applied at the subject level and to training subjects only: in each replicate a seeded draw flips the label of 0%, 10% or 20% of the training subjects, validation and test subjects keep their clean labels, and the clean label is stored beside the observed one (https://doi.org/10.1109/TNNLS.2013.2292894 [verified: 2026-10-25]). Because the generator yields independent subjects without limit, E5 replaces cross-validation by fresh subjects: each replicate trains on its 60 training subjects, stops early on its 10 validation subjects and is scored once on its 100 test subjects, none of them shared with another replicate; E1 on the declared dataset keeps its subject-wise cross-validation. The fixed regime runs the monotone rule at each separation without label noise and at d′ = 1.5 with 10% and 20% label noise, and the interaction and frequency-two rules with 0% and 20% label noise, each condition on 8 replicates (generator seeds 1 to 8); the end-to-end regime, which only checks the pipeline, runs the monotone rule at d′ = 3.0 and d′ = 1.5 without label noise and at d′ = 1.5 with 20% label noise, each on 5 replicates (generator seeds 1 to 5). Replicates share no data, so their scores are independent, and arms are compared by the Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the paired replicates, whose smallest exact two-sided p with 8 replicates is about 0.008. The confirmatory family is the Hybrid DRU against the Single-shot arm on the frequency-two rule at 0% and 20% label noise, Holm-corrected, with the rank-biserial correlation and a bootstrap interval over replicates; each quantum arm against the linear probe on the interaction rule is reported as an exploratory family, also Holm-adjusted. Robustness is reported per arm and regime as the slope of subject-level Balanced Accuracy on the label-noise rate with its 95% interval; it describes, and tests nothing.
- **Success criterion:** In the fixed regime on the frequency-two rule without label noise, the Hybrid DRU's subject-level Balanced Accuracy is higher than the Single-shot arm's by the Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the 8 paired replicates with Holm-adjusted p < 0.05; with the monotone rule and no label noise, each arm's mean subject-level Balanced Accuracy in both regimes lies within 0.05 of the ceiling Φ(d′/2) (pipeline check); and in the fixed regime the linear probe's mean subject-level Balanced Accuracy on the interaction and frequency-two rules stays at or below 0.675, the linear ceiling plus 0.05 (construction check: a higher value means the front end is not fixed).
- **Priority:** ablation
- **Estimated Cost:** About 5.9 CPU hours before early stopping. ## Threats to Validity and Mitigation
- **Subject Correlation (Data Leakage):** Mitigated by strict StratifiedGroupKFold on subject IDs. Adjacent overlapping epochs of the same child never cross split partitions.
- **Quantum Circuit Noise / Simulation Artifacts:** Verified in state-vector simulation mode; subsequent noise-model evaluation accounts for depolarization and readout errors.
- **Class Imbalance:** Retained 120 subjects present an exactly balanced 60/60 distribution, preventing majority-class diagnostic bias.
- **Dependent Fold Scores:** Cross-validation folds share training subjects, so fold scores are not independent; the corrected resampled t-test and the Friedman test over fold-by-seed blocks account for it, and no test is run over pooled epochs.
- **Multiple Comparisons:** Each experiment tests a family of hypotheses; Holm-Bonferroni correction keeps the family-wise error at alpha.
- **Validation Scheme Choice:** Leave-one-subject-out validation is argued for EEG disease classification (https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/the-necessity-of-leave-one-subject-out-loso-cross-validation-for-/ [verified: 2026-10-24]); the stratified group split is also subject-independent and is kept for cost, and the choice is reported.
- **Under-trained Pilot:** The first pilot stopped improving only because its 30-epoch budget ended; the maximum of 100 epochs with early stopping and the repeated pilot ensure the full runs are not judged on an under-converged model.
- **Selection on Validation Subjects:** Hyperparameters chosen on the same subjects that report the score would inflate it; the search is nested inside each outer fold and never sees its test subjects, and every arm receives the same budget.
- **Hardware Differences Across Shards:** Different accelerators produce different floating-point results; a campaign runs on one service and one accelerator model, and shards whose environment stamps differ are not merged.
- **Backbone Absorbing the Nonlinearity:** A backbone trained end to end can learn a nonlinear class rule by itself, which would hide any difference between readouts; E5's fixed front end removes the trainable backbone so that only the readout differs between arms.
- **Synthetic Data Are Not EEG:** E5 checks capacity and robustness under a known generator; it sustains no clinical claim and never replaces E1 on the declared dataset.
- **Ceilings Depend on the Generator:** The rule ceilings hold for noiseless latents and trial noise lowers them; they are reported beside each arm as upper bounds, and the generator is versioned with every toy set.
- **Noisy Test Labels:** Label noise in test subjects would measure agreement with the noise rather than accuracy; E5 flips training labels only and stores the clean label beside the observed one.
- **Fresh Test Subjects Instead of Cross-validation:** Scoring on newly generated subjects is valid only because the data are synthetic and the generator yields independent subjects; it is never carried to E1, which keeps subject-wise cross-validation on the declared dataset.
- **Under-training under Fixed Settings:** E5 runs no search, so a budget that under-trains the fixed-regime readouts would pass for a capacity limit; the v05 run showed it (see the Protocol Amendment of E5), and the amended budget was selected on separate generator seeds by validation loss and convergence, never on the test subjects of E5's own seeds. The pipeline check and the convergence curves still expose any remaining under-training before a comparison is read.
- **Amendment after a Run:** Changing a training budget after seeing a run invites a forking-paths objection; the amendment changes only the fixed regime's budget, applies it identically to every arm, leaves the success criterion, its tolerance, the ceilings and the confirmatory family unchanged, and reports the v05 run beside the rerun.
- **Sample Size of the Frequency-two Rule:** With 60 training subjects each of the rule's four arcs holds about 15 subjects; a readout that fails it may lack data rather than capacity, which is why the training set was not reduced below 60.

## Excluded Alternatives
- **Random Epoch Splitting:** Excluded because it violates clinical validation standards and artificially inflates metrics due to intra-subject autocorrelation.
- **Single-qubit Quantum Classifiers:** Excluded due to restricted Hilbert space dimensionality and inability to model multipartite feature entanglement.
- **Trial-level Label Noise:** Excluded because labels are per subject; flipping single epochs breaks the one-label-per-subject property the subject-level metrics need.
- **A Non-monotone Single-band Rule:** Excluded because a degree-one trigonometric readout already represents an even rule (the cosine is even), so it cannot separate L = 3 from L = 1; the frequency-two rule can.

## 1. Dataset Overview and Clinical Cohort

- **Repository / Source:** IEEE DataPort (DOI: 10.21227/rzpv-9r35)

## 2. Electrophysiological Acquisition Parameters

## 3. Standard Preprocessing Protocol
- **Filtering:** 
  - 50 Hz notch filter for power-line interference suppression.
  - 0.5–60 Hz zero-phase band-pass filter (Butterworth 4th order) to isolate physiological rhythms (Delta, Theta, Alpha, Beta) and suppress low-frequency drift and high-frequency muscular noise.

## 4. Methodological Bounds and Experimental Constraints
- **Subject-Independent Partitioning:** All epochs from a single subject must reside exclusively within either the training set, the validation set, or the test set. Random epoch-level shuffling constitutes data leakage due to high intra-subject correlation across overlapping epochs.
- **Evaluation Level:** Evaluation must be reported at both the epoch level and the aggregated subject level (majority voting or soft posterior probability averaging across all epochs of each subject).

# Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Network with Data Re-uploading for EEG-based ADHD Classification

## 1. Problem Formulation and Clinical Scope

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) diagnosis traditionally relies on behavioral questionnaires, which are subject to clinical observer bias. Electroencephalography (EEG) provides a high-temporal-resolution physiological measurement of brain electrical activity. Under the international 10-20 standard montage, multichannel recordings capture rhythmic oscillations across standardized frequency bands: Delta (0.5-4 Hz), Theta (4-8 Hz), Alpha (8-12 Hz), and Beta (12-30 Hz). In pediatric ADHD, elevated Theta power alongside reduced Beta activity in fronto-central electrodes constitutes the established Theta/Beta ratio (TBR) biomarker.

However, multichannel EEG suffers from substantial inter-subject variability, low signal-to-noise ratio, and volume conduction artifacts. This proposal formulates a hybrid quantum-classical learning architecture wherein a classical convolutional backbone condenses spatial-temporal correlations into a compact latent vector, and a parameterized quantum circuit with circular Data Re-uploading (DRU) operates as an expressive non-linear classifier.

## 2. Classical Spatial-Temporal Feature Extraction

Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times T}$ denote a raw EEG epoch segment comprising $C$ recording channels (with $C = 19$ under the 10-20 system) and $T$ discrete temporal sampling points. The classical feature extraction backbone $f_{\phi}: \mathbb{R}^{C \times T} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ maps the continuous EEG epoch into a low-dimensional feature representation matching the qubit width of the quantum register:

$$
x = f_{\phi}(X) \in [-\pi, \pi]^d, \quad \text{with } d = N_q = 4
\tag{1}
$$

The dimension $d$ is deliberately constrained to $N_q = 4$ to permit direct qubit allocation without barren plateaus. The four latent dimensions correspond to distinct physiological features: frontal Theta power, central Beta power, global Theta/Beta ratio, and inter-hemispheric frontal coherence.

## 3. Quantum Data Re-uploading Classifier Architecture

### 3.1 Data Re-uploading Unitary Operator

Rather than employing a single-shot state preparation circuit, the quantum classifier interleaves data encoding blocks with trainable variational transformations across $L$ successive layers. The global unitary evolution operator $U(x, \theta)$ acting on the reference state $|0^{\otimes N_q}\rangle$ is defined as:

$$
U(x, \theta) = \prod_{l=1}^L W_l(\theta_l) \, S_l(x)
\tag{2}
$$

The data encoding operator $S_l(x)$ applies single-qubit rotations parameterized by affine transformations of the input vector $x$, where $\alpha_{l,j}$ and $\beta_{l,j}$ are layer-specific scaling and bias parameters:

$$
S_l(x) = \bigotimes_{j=1}^{N_q} R_y(\alpha_{l,j} x_j + \beta_{l,j})
\tag{3}
$$

Each variational layer $W_l(\theta_l)$ applies parameterized single-qubit rotations followed by an entangling operation:

$$
W_l(\theta_l) = V_{\text{ent}} \cdot \bigotimes_{j=1}^{N_q} R_z(\theta_{l,j}^{(2)}) R_y(\theta_{l,j}^{(1)})
\tag{4}
$$

### 3.2 Circular Entanglement Topology

To ensure non-local correlations and maximum bipartite entanglement across the 4-qubit register while preventing boundary asymmetry, $V_{\text{ent}}$ is configured with a closed periodic boundary (circular CNOT ring):

$$
V_{\text{ent}} = \left( \prod_{j=1}^{N_q - 1} \text{CNOT}_{j, j+1} \right) \cdot \text{CNOT}_{N_q, 1}
\tag{5}
$$

By virtue of the $L$-fold re-uploading defined in (Ec. 2), the quantum circuit acts as a truncated Fourier series whose accessible spectrum extends up to harmonic degree $L$.

### 3.3 Measurement and Decision Boundary

Expectation values of the Pauli-$Z$ observable are measured individually on each qubit $j \in \{1, \dots, N_q\}$:

$$
\langle Z_j \rangle(x, \theta) = \langle 0^{\otimes N_q} | U^\dagger(x, \theta) \, Z_j \, U(x, \theta) | 0^{\otimes N_q} \rangle \in [-1, 1]
\tag{6}
$$

The vector of expectation values $\langle Z \rangle = [\langle Z_1 \rangle, \dots, \langle Z_{N_q} \rangle]^T$ is projected onto a binary diagnostic decision through a classical linear combination followed by the logistic sigmoid activation:

$$
\hat{y} = \sigma\left( \sum_{j=1}^{N_q} w_j \langle Z_j \rangle(x, \theta) + b \right)
\tag{7}
$$

where $\hat{y} \in (0, 1)$ represents the predicted posterior probability of ADHD diagnosis ($y = 1$) versus healthy control ($y = 0$).

## 4. Loss Function and Parameter Optimization

The joint parameters of the hybrid network, including the classical weights $\phi$, the quantum rotation parameters $\theta = \{\theta_l\}_{l=1}^L$, the encoding scales $\alpha, \beta$, and the output weights $w, b$, are trained end-to-end via regularized binary cross-entropy:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\phi, \theta, w, b) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ y^{(i)} \log \hat{y}^{(i)} + (1 - y^{(i)}) \log(1 - \hat{y}^{(i)}) \right] + \lambda \|\theta\|_2^2
\tag{8}
$$

Optimization proceeds using classical gradient descent with the parameter-shift rule applied to obtain exact analytical gradients for all quantum circuit parameters.

## 5. Clinical Interpretability via Analytical Gradients

To verify clinical validity, the decision saliency with respect to the input EEG signal is derived analytically using the chain rule across the classical and quantum layers:

$$
\mathcal{I}(c, t) = \left| \sum_{k=1}^{N_q} \frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial x_k} \frac{\partial f_{\phi}(X)_k}{\partial X_{c,t}} \right|
\tag{9}
$$

Because the partial derivatives $\frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial x_k}$ are computed via (Ec. 7) and the parameter-shift rule on (Ec. 6), the saliency map $\mathcal{I}(c, t)$ exposes the precise contribution of each electrode $c$ and time sample $t$. This provides a direct clinical audit mechanism to confirm that the hybrid model leverages physiologically established fronto-central regions (Fp1, Fp2, F3, F4, Fz, Cz) rather than background noise artifacts.
